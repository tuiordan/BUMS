RepRight User Research

Interview 5

Interview Information

Interview Date

October 14, 2026

Interviewer

Ezekiel Opie

Participant Description

Intermediate Lifter

Workout Background

1. How often do you work out, and what types of exercises do you typically perform?

I typically work out five to six times per week. I aim to lift weights three times per week, while the other two to three days are dedicated to walking, running, stretching, or improving mobility.

2. How would you describe your experience level with weightlifting or strength training (beginner, intermediate, or advanced)?

I would describe my weightlifting experience as intermediate.

3. Have you ever struggled with performing an exercise correctly or knowing whether your form was good?

Yes. This was especially challenging when I first started lifting because I was not always sure where to begin or which movements would help me reach my fitness goals.

Current Challenges

4. How do you currently check or improve your exercise form?

I often check my form by watching instructional videos. I also improve my technique by working out with someone who is more experienced, checking my form in a mirror, or recording myself on my phone.

5. What is your biggest frustration when it comes to maintaining proper exercise technique?

My biggest frustration occurs when I introduce a new movement into my workout routine. Poor technique can cause me to strain something, such as my back, or lead to an injury that affects my daily routine.

App and Feature Ideas

6. If an app could analyze videos of your exercises and provide feedback on your form, how useful would that be to you, and why?

I think it would be beneficial because correcting my form early could help me create better workout routines and see results more quickly than if my technique remained incorrect.

7. What features would you want in an app like RepRight?

I would like an option for a live fitness coach that works similarly to a client call, possibly as a subscription feature. I would also find it useful to receive information about the science behind my goals, such as muscle growth or weight loss, including guidance about nutrition and water intake.

8. Would you prefer real-time feedback while exercising or feedback after uploading a recorded video? Why?

I think both types of feedback would be useful for different reasons. Real-time feedback would feel more personal and could help users feel encouraged when they are improving or performing well. Feedback from uploaded recordings would also be useful because users could review the video again and clearly see what the feedback is referring to.

Concerns and Priorities

9. What concerns would you have about using an AI-powered workout form analysis app (such as accuracy, privacy, cost, or ease of use)?

I would have concerns about relying on exercise critiques without in-person correction. For example, AI may be able to identify posture changes, but it may have difficulty adjusting exercises based on a user's unique anatomical limitations. It may also be difficult for the app to determine whether someone is sacrificing proper technique simply to lift heavier weight.

10. What is the one feature that would make you most likely to use RepRight regularly?

I would be most likely to use RepRight regularly if it could explain how different techniques or variations in form affect which muscles are being targeted. For example, changing foot placement can change which muscle group is emphasized during an exercise.

Additional Preferences

11. Which exercises would you most want RepRight to analyze first, and why?

I think RepRight should first analyze compound movements such as back squats, deadlifts, pull-ups, and bench presses. When performed correctly, these exercises can effectively target multiple muscle groups. However, they can also lead to serious injuries if they are performed with poor form.

12. What kind of feedback would be easiest for you to understand (written tips, visual overlays, scores, or video demonstrations)?

Video demonstrations and visual overlays showing the muscle groups being used would be the easiest forms of feedback for me to understand.

13. Would you find it helpful to track your exercise form improvements over time? What would you want to see?

Yes. I think a rating scale from one to ten would be useful for tracking form improvements over time. The score could take into account how consistently I have performed the exercise and the progress I have made with increasing weight.

14. Where and how would you most likely use RepRight (at home, at the gym, before a workout, or afterward)?

I think RepRight could be useful in almost any setting. It could be especially helpful for people like me who prefer working out at home, as well as people who want to start lifting but find crowded gyms intimidating when they are still unfamiliar with proper form.

15. Would you consider paying for RepRight? If so, what would make a paid version worthwhile?

Yes, I would consider paying for RepRight if the platform included dietary information or personal training plans tailored to my individual goals.

Additional Comments

No additional comments.
